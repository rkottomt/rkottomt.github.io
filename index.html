About Me

Sophomore Student studying ECE at Carnegie Mellon University
01 About

I'm Rohit, an Electrical and Computer Engineering student at Carnegie Mellon University (Dean's List, Class of 2029). I grew up at the #1 STEM high school in the country and arrived at CMU with a deep interest in the hardware-software boundary.

My work spans optics hardware, machine learning, and systems engineering. I've researched beehive acoustic anomaly detection with an IIT ECE professor, built ML classification models at Pfizer, and helped launch the first high school chapter of Hack4Impact. This summer I'm joining Nokia Bell Labs as an Optics Hardware Intern.

Outside of engineering I think about energy systems and environmental research — the intersection of physical infrastructure and climate. I'm also drawn to low-latency systems, quantitative finance, and anything that rewards careful reasoning under uncertainty. If you're building something at the edge of the physical and computational, reach out.

02 Experience

Specialized in FPGA design, PCB design, and GPU optimization for real-world Distributed Acoustic Sensing systems. Optimized circuit test procedures using a flying probe machine to improve efficiency in PCB validation workflows. Troubleshot and repaired faulty circuit packs and ethernet cables to support hardware testing and system reliability.

Collaborated with two teammates to build machine learning models for software access classification. The models were successfully integrated into Pfizer workflows, helping streamline access decisions for critical software and hardware systems.

Contributing to ScottyLabs, a student-run organization that builds technology to improve campus life at Carnegie Mellon. Currently developing Labrador, an alumni networking platform designed to connect CMU students with alumni across industries.

Serving on the executive board for Build18, Carnegie Mellon’s largest ECE buildathon. Responsible for managing sponsorship outreach with notable companies such as Blue Origin and sourcing equipment to support student hardware projects.

Collaborated with an ECE professor to develop a machine learning system that identified anomalies in beehive acoustics. The project helped advance research on bee behavior and environmental responses to climate change. Deployed the model in a real-world setting by building an Arduino-based electrical system for data collection and testing.

Founded the first high school chapter of Hack4Impact, bringing the organization’s university-based nonprofit software model to High Technology High School. Led student teams in building technology solutions for environmental nonprofits, including an OCR-powered research database for the Hudson River Foundation. Secured additional project opportunities with The Nature Conservancy focused on wildlife conservation, shark tracking, and owl migration mapping.

Joined KronML as a founding machine learning intern at an early-stage AI startup. Designed and implemented the company’s intern onboarding and development process. Built a long-term machine learning automation pipeline to reduce repetitive manual operations.

Researched the use of AI in large-scale data centers and its implications for infrastructure, regulation, and governance. Contributed to drafting legislative language for a proposed New Jersey state bill focused on AI oversight and responsible data center development.
